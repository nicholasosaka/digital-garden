Increasingly, AI generated content is being used to substite human work. Strictly speaking, it's not new for things like social media comments. But it is new for blog posts, college essays, and work documents. We're also learning how to identify generated text quickly: just look for turns of phrases like "Here's what most people miss," or "It's not X, it's Y," or maybe even "Not X. Not Y. But Z." I dislike reading AI generated text, and I suspect that's a not-unpopular stance. I crave authentic words, written by a real human. I don't care if it's difficult to read or hard to understand, I just want to know that I'm reading the words someone **wrote**. It's an arms race though, between my desire for authentic human words and the companies who strive for *their* model to appear human enough to trick all of us who stop reading if it's AI generated. In a way, we're both chasing the authentic mark of what it means to write like a human. Imperfections, odd word choice, or even the flow of writing that's a bit off balance. 

What do you value more, human written language or AI generated text?[^1] Personally, I will always value the work and effort that went into writing something over the frictionless act of prompting an LLM. That's the rheotical engine behind LLMs, right? That what was once arduious will become frictionless for all of us. I don't see the point of doing anything if there's not some friction to it. There's no accomplishment without friction. The future is a world where passivity wins, and I think to avoid that world it's imperative for us to chase authenticity. Authenticity means putting in a bit of effort, and even if it's less 'perfect' than were you to prompt an LLM, I promise it's not. The perfection of your work comes from the fact that *you* wrote it. Nothing can usurp you when it comes to being human.
